### run800

**Hypothesis:** Can ONE policy track 10 different loops over the Camp Buckner topology when it never observes which loop is active? Synthetic ideal-binary detection, loop sampled uniformly per episode, tl=3, max_sensors=6 of 10, sensor_rew=-0.25, PPO from scratch, plateau early-stop (patience 30 evals, min_delta 0.5pp, cap 600).

**Findings:** Partial success with a WIDE per-loop spread. Best at iteration 20 of 170: mean 63.49% @ 5.51 sensors/step vs untrained baseline 32.12% @ 5.99 - so the policy roughly doubles baseline accuracy while using slightly fewer sensors. But per-loop accuracy ranges 28.30% (loop 0) to 89.00% (loop 3), sd 18.62pp: the single agent does NOT cover the family evenly. Loop length is not the driver (corr +0.20; the 10-node Hamiltonian loop 3 is the EASIEST); successor ambiguity correlates negatively but only moderately (corr -0.41). Loops 0 and 2 differ by one transposition yet score 28.30% vs 42.30%, so the policy resolves the family inconsistently. Training then DECAYED monotonically after iter 20: by iter 170 accuracy 35.22% at 3.22 sensors - both axes worse together, which is a too-strong penalty rather than an energy trade. Reward arithmetic explains it: at sensor_rew=-0.25 with the 6-sensor cap, detecting with all 6 on nets exactly 0.00 (cost 1.5 cancels reward 1.5) while a 1-sensor hit nets +1.25, so PPO chases a precision it cannot achieve under ambiguity. Cheap training eval (5 eps/loop) gave 63.50% at iter 20 vs 63.49% on the thorough 20 eps/loop re-measure - the cheap-eval design is sound. NEXT: sweep --sensor-rew (0.0, -0.05, -0.10) from scratch to find the real ceiling and a usable operating point. See diagnostics.md.

**Hypothesis:** LEVER B (sensor_rew -0.16 -> -2.00). Every activation strictly negative-value; included to bracket the arm.

**Findings:** 53.58% @ 5.44 sensors, sd 26.56pp, worst loop 0.30%. A 12.5x steepening from -0.16 buys only -0.25 sensors/step. PREDICTION REFUTED: I expected collapse toward inactivity.
